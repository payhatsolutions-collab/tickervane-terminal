NSE stock delivery analysis with Delivery Radar

Alpha Nova’s Delivery Radar compares the latest session’s delivered quantity and delivery percentage with each stock’s own recent baseline. It helps you spot activity that deserves a closer look after the market closes.

What is delivery quantity?

Delivery quantity is the portion of traded shares marked for delivery rather than intraday square-off. Delivery percentage compares that quantity with total traded volume. A large delivered quantity can reflect genuine position changes, but it does not identify who bought or sold, or why.

How does the radar find unusual activity?

The radar uses NSE’s security-wise delivery bhavcopy. For stocks with enough observations, it compares the latest delivered quantity with the preceding 20 sessions. A delivery spike is at least 1.8 times that average. You can filter by Nifty 500 membership, watchlist, turnover, company and industry.

What do the labels mean?

  • Accumulation: a delivery spike with an up close near the upper part of the day’s range.
  • Distribution: a delivery spike with a down close near the lower part of the range.
  • High conviction: delivery percentage above 60% and at least 25% above that stock’s normal level.

These labels describe patterns in price and delivery data. They do not prove institutional buying, selling pressure or future price direction.

When is the data available?

NSE publishes delivery data after the trading session, so the latest result is end-of-day rather than live. Alpha Nova shows the session date and source beside the radar. Public data can be delayed, corrected or temporarily unavailable.
